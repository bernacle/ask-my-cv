# Technical Leadership

## Mentoring And Team Support

Bruno has helped teammates through code reviews, technical discussions, pairing, onboarding, and sharing context about systems.

At Banco do Brasil, he led developers while working on an Open Finance developer platform. At Notabene, he supported startup growth by participating in hiring, interviewing, onboarding, and mentoring new engineers.

He tries to mentor in a practical way: explaining context, discussing tradeoffs, and helping people become more confident in the system.

## Technical Leadership Without Formal Management

Most of Bruno's leadership has been technical leadership.

He has owned initiatives around integrations, backend services, API design, authentication migration, identity flows, customer dashboards, and production improvements.

For Bruno, technical leadership means understanding the problem, proposing a good path, aligning with others, breaking down the work, and making sure the solution ships.

## Code Reviews

Bruno sees code reviews as a way to improve quality and share knowledge.

He looks for correctness, readability, maintainability, edge cases, tests, and whether the implementation really solves the product requirement.

He tries to be clear about what is important and what is just a preference. If something is a real issue, he explains the risk. If something is optional, he says that too.

## Technical Decisions

Bruno helps teams make technical decisions by making tradeoffs clear.

He usually compares options based on complexity, delivery time, risk, maintainability, and future flexibility. He likes practical decisions that fit the product stage and the team.

He tries not to choose technology just because it is trendy. He prefers tools and designs that solve the actual problem well.

## Process Improvements

At Notabene, Bruno helped improve how integrations and provider-specific behavior were organized.

When a product supports many external providers, the code can easily become full of special cases. Bruno helped make provider differences more explicit through clearer mappings, configuration, and fallback behavior.

That made the system easier to extend and easier for other engineers to understand.
