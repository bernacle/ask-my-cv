# Working Style

## Approach To Ambiguous Problems

Bruno usually starts ambiguous technical problems by understanding the real goal.

Before jumping into implementation, he asks what problem is being solved, who the user is, what constraints exist, what risks matter, and what the smallest useful version could be.

Then he breaks the problem into smaller parts and identifies what is known, what is unknown, and what assumptions are being made. He prefers simple solutions first, while avoiding shortcuts that will clearly become painful later.

## Balancing Speed, Quality, And Maintainability

Bruno tries to be pragmatic when balancing speed, quality, and maintainability.

Not every feature needs complex architecture, but production code should be understandable, safe, and easy to change. He cares about clear naming, good boundaries, useful tests, and avoiding unnecessary abstractions.

When speed matters, he is comfortable making tradeoffs, but he prefers to make them explicit. For example, he may identify something as good enough for a first version while noting which part should be revisited if usage grows.

## Communicating Tradeoffs

Bruno communicates tradeoffs in terms of user impact, delivery time, risk, and maintenance cost.

Instead of only saying something is not scalable or technically wrong, he explains what could happen in practice: slower delivery later, harder debugging, more operational risk, or a worse user experience.

He usually presents options clearly, such as a faster option, a safer option, and sometimes a more complete long-term option.

## What Teammates Rely On Bruno For

Teammates usually rely on Bruno for ownership, technical judgment, backend design, API design, debugging, and connecting product requirements with implementation.

He is often able to take an unclear problem, ask useful questions, propose a path, and ship it. Because he has worked across backend, frontend, product, and infrastructure-adjacent areas, he can usually understand how a change affects the full system.

## Engineering Culture

Bruno does his best work in a culture with trust, ownership, and direct communication.

He likes when engineers are involved early in product discussions, not only after everything has already been decided. He also likes teams that care about quality without becoming slow or bureaucratic.

The best environment for Bruno is collaborative, pragmatic, and honest about tradeoffs.
